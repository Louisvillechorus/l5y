    Buttons

    Scenes

    A scene is a saved mix of the background layers (0 to 100). Cues switch scenes and the board glides between them over the fade time. Tension is a throttle the operator rides with = and -; a scene only moves it if you type a number in its Tension column (blank means leave it where it is). Street life (the random cars, dogs and horns) is not part of a scene: it is one slider on the Show tab that stays where you put it and only plays while the street or rain is up.

    Sounds

    Click a take to hear it; the highlighted take is the one the show uses. Level, position (left to right as the audience sees the stage), space (how much room it sits in) and dip (how far the atmosphere ducks while it plays) are all yours to change. Use my own file swaps in any MP3 or WAV.

    Output

    One stereo cable from the laptop headphone jack into two channels on the house board, panned hard left and hard right. Laptop volume at 100%; set the level on the board, not here.

    How to run the show

    Read once before the first rehearsal. The stage manager calls every cue; you never decide timing on your own.

    Before house opens

    1. Laptop plugged in, sleep turned off, volume at 100%.
    2. Open this page and press Load sounds.
    3. Setup tab: Line check. You should hear a tone left, then right, then noise in the middle. Set the board so the noise sits at a comfortable house level.
    4. Show tab: Reset to top. Standby shows cue 1. Type the page the SM starts on into Script page.

    During the show

    • Space or the GO button fires the cue in Standby. The standby card shows exactly what will happen.
    • 1 to 0 fire the buttons. Phone keeps ringing until you press again.
    • = and - are the tension throttle: each press nudges the Tension bed up or down. It is yours to ride; cues never touch it. 0 (Punctuation) hits the metal and cuts the tension dead.
    • [ and ] flip the script page. Buttons called on that page light amber. GO moves the page to the cue you just fired.
    • ↑ / ↓ move Standby without playing anything.
    • Esc fades everything out over 2 seconds.
    • Clicking a cue puts it in Standby. It does not fire it.

    Cues that land with lights

    Some cues are marked with lights. The SM calls "lights and sound, GO" and both operators press on the word. Our sound starts within about 15 milliseconds of the key, so there is no lead to build in: press on GO, not before.

    If something goes wrong

    • Missed a cue: press GO as soon as you can. Late is better than skipped.
    • Fired early: press Esc only if it is clearly wrong; the scene fades out.
    • Page reloaded: press Load sounds, click the cue the SM is on, press Rehearse from here, keep going.
    • Rehearsal jumps: click the cue, press Rehearse from here. The mix snaps to where the show would be, nothing else plays.
    • No sound: check the cable, the laptop volume, the Out pill (should say Stereo).

    Where sounds sit

    Every sound has a position left to right as the audience sees the stage: the hall door and the street door on the left, the phone a little right of center, the windows and the fridge on the right. Music and the announcement sit in the middle. Offstage sounds carry a little stairwell echo; room sounds a little room. Change any of it on the Sounds tab.

    Street life

    The random cars, dogs, horns, buses and far sirens are one slider in Atmosphere. It is not a cue: set it once in tech and leave it. They only play while the street or rain bed is up, so they stop on their own in the silent scenes. Every drop has four recordings and never plays the same one twice in a row, at a slightly different speed, level and spot each time, and never two within twenty seconds.

    Changing the show in tech

    Move the Atmosphere sliders until the director is happy, then Save to scene. Add, reorder or rewrite cues on the Cues tab. Change takes, levels and positions on the Sounds tab. Then copy the show file from Setup as a backup.
