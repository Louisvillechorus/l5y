    Scenes

    A scene is a saved mix of the background layers (0 to 100). Cues switch scenes and the board glides between them over the fade time. Tension is a throttle the operator rides with = and -; a scene only moves it if you type a number in its Tension column (blank means leave it where it is). Street life (the random cars, dogs and horns) is not part of a scene: it is one slider on the Show tab that stays where you put it and only plays while the street or rain is up. The heartbeat is the operator's too (H, then , and . for the pace); scenes never start or stop it.

    Sounds

    Click a take to hear it; the highlighted take is the one the show uses. Level, position (left to right as the audience sees the stage), space (how much room it sits in) and dip (how far the atmosphere ducks while it plays) are all yours to change. Use my own file swaps in any MP3 or WAV.

    Show file

    Everything you have changed (cues, scenes, buttons, levels, takes) in one block of text. Copy it after tech and paste it somewhere safe. Load it on the booth laptop to move the whole show.

    To move the show to the other iMac: Download show file here, carry it over (AirDrop or a thumb drive), then Open show file there. Sounds you added from your own files stay on the computer you added them on.

    Shared show

    Connect every booth computer to the same shared show. A change saves on this computer first, then reaches everyone else's board within about 15 seconds. If the internet drops, the board keeps running on this computer's copy and catches up when it is back. During a performance, other people's changes wait until the operator chooses to load them.

    How to run the show

    Read once before the first rehearsal. The stage manager calls every cue; you never decide timing on your own.

    Before house opens

    1. Laptop plugged in, sleep turned off, volume at 100%.
    2. Open this page and press Load sounds.
    3. Setup tab: Line check. You should hear a tone left, then right, then noise in the middle. Set the board so the noise sits at a comfortable house level.
    4. Show tab: Reset to top. Standby shows cue 1. Type the page the SM starts on into Script page.

    During the show

    • Space or the GO button fires the cue in Standby. The standby card shows exactly what will happen.
    • 1 to 0 fire the buttons. Phone keeps ringing until you press again. 9 (Flourish) plays a different one each press. 0 is the hit.
    • = and - are the tension throttle: each press nudges the Tension bed up or down. It is yours to ride; cues never touch it, and in the last scene it sits on top of the Roat underscore so you can make it ebb and flow with him. P (Punctuation) hits the metal and cuts the tension dead.
    • H starts and stops the heartbeat. , and . slow it down and speed it up (the Heartbeat slider does the same); it gets a little louder as it races. P (Punctuation) cuts it dead along with the tension. Built for the final fight: start it on the light shift, slow it with the slow motion (under 60 it sinks lower, like the world slowing down), race it toward the knife, then K.
    • K is the stab: one sound used nowhere else in the show. It cuts the heartbeat and the tension dead in the same instant.
    • D plays a few notes of the doll's tune over the Scene 1 under, for each time the doll comes up on pages 10 to 14 (the first one is a cue).
    • The underscores in Act Two (strings at the window, the piano when Mike comes back, the pulse under Mike and Susan, the Roat underscore) are scenes: a GO brings each in and a later GO takes it out. They are on the Atmosphere faders while they play if the director wants one nudged.
    • A cue marked practical is one sound tied to one thing an actor does. Fire it on the action, as it happens. Nothing on this board is chained or on a timer: if two things happen on stage, they are two GOs.
    • [ and ] flip the script page. Buttons called on that page light amber. GO moves the page to the cue you just fired.
    • ↑ / ↓ move Standby without playing anything.
    • Esc fades everything out over 2 seconds.
    • Clicking a cue puts it in Standby. It does not fire it.

    Cues that land with lights

    Some cues are marked with lights. The SM calls "lights and sound, GO" and both operators press on the word. Our sound starts within about 15 milliseconds of the key, so there is no lead to build in: press on GO, not before.

    If something goes wrong

    • Missed a cue: press GO as soon as you can. Late is better than skipped.
    • Fired early: press Esc only if it is clearly wrong; the scene fades out.
    • Page reloaded: press Load sounds, click the cue the SM is on, press Rehearse from here, keep going.
    • Rehearsal jumps: click the cue, press Rehearse from here. The mix snaps to where the show would be, nothing else plays.
    • No sound: check the cable, the laptop volume, the Out pill (should say Stereo).

    Where sounds sit

    Every sound has a position left to right as the audience sees the stage: the hall door and the street door on the left, the phone a little right of center, the windows and the fridge on the right. Music and the announcement sit in the middle. Offstage sounds carry a little stairwell echo; room sounds a little room. Change any of it on the Sounds tab.

    Street life

    The random cars, dogs, horns, buses and far sirens are one slider in Atmosphere. It is not a cue: set it once in tech and leave it. They only play while the street or rain bed is up, so they stop on their own in the silent scenes. Every drop has four recordings and never plays the same one twice in a row, at a slightly different speed, level and spot each time, and never two within twenty seconds.

    Heartbeat

    One beat per file, three takes, played on a tempo the operator rides from 30 to 180 beats a minute. It is not a cue and no scene touches it: H starts it, the slider or , and . set the pace, H fades it out over a second, and the Punctuation hit cuts it dead in the same instant it cuts the tension. Slow is quiet and, under 60, lower and heavier, as if time were slowing; fast is a few decibels louder. K (the stab) cuts it dead. Every beat is a slightly different take at a slightly different pitch, so it never sounds like a loop.

    Changing the show in tech

    Move the Atmosphere sliders until the director is happy, then Save to scene. Add, reorder or rewrite cues on the Cues tab. Change takes, levels and positions on the Sounds tab. Then Download show file from Setup as a backup.

    Saving

    Every change saves on this computer the moment you make it; the top bar shows the time of the last save. Reloading the page, closing Chrome or restarting the iMac keeps everything. The one exception is the Atmosphere sliders: moving them changes the sound right now, and Save to scene is what keeps it. Use the same computer and Chrome every night. When the booth computers are connected to the shared show (Setup, Shared show), a change on one reaches the others within about 15 seconds, and nothing changes on a board mid-performance unless its operator loads it. Without that connection, Download show file on one computer and Open show file on the other. If something gets deleted by mistake, Setup, Earlier versions puts it back.
